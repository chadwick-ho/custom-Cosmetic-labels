Label Size Guide

How to Choose the Right Label Size for Bottles

Updated October 17, 2026 by YT Labels

Measure the package you will actually label, then make a paper fit test before approving a dieline. This guide is for cosmetic, shampoo, body wash and similar bottle projects.

AI-generated packaging illustration; not a factory photograph or a measured fit-test result.

Start with the filled container, not a stock bottle size

A 250 ml bottle does not have one universal label size. The shoulder, base curve, recessed grip, cap and straight sidewall decide where a label can sit. Fit the pump or cap, mark the usable panel on the actual pack and measure its height. Leave clearance where the surface bends or the customer grips the bottle.

If I were preparing a quote for a pump bottle and a matching squeeze bottle, I would send two measured panels. One shared artwork style is sensible; one untested dieline for two different shapes is not.

Decide between a front label and a wrap label

For a front-only label, measure the width across the visible front panel and the height between the shoulder and base curve. A front-and-back pair gives product identity and detailed copy separate spaces. A wrap can carry more information, but the seam and any barcode need a deliberate position.

For a straight cylindrical bottle, measure circumference with a flexible tape at the proposed label height. If you only have the diameter, a preliminary circumference is π × diameter. Subtract your chosen seam gap to estimate wrap width. For example, a 50 mm diameter and a 3 mm gap give about 154.1 mm of label width. This is an illustration, not a recommended production size. Use the wrap label size calculator to test your own numbers; it does not add bleed or create a print-ready dieline.

Test the width and height on paper

  1. Mark the intended top, bottom and side edges on the real bottle.
  2. Cut plain paper to the proposed width and height, then wrap or place it on the container.
  3. Check the front view, seam gap, cap clearance, shoulder, base curve and any grip area.
  4. Photograph the applied paper from the front and side, and record the final measurements in mm or inches.

A digital rectangle can look right while a paper mockup crosses a curved shoulder. Avery's cut-and-wrap measuring method also recommends testing a paper shape on the product before ordering.

When a rectangle will not fit

If the top and bottom of the proposed label panel have different circumferences, the bottle is tapered. Do not stretch a rectangular wrap around it in artwork and assume the edges will align. Measure both circumferences and the label-panel height, then request a tapered dieline and a full-size paper test. The OnlineLabels tapered-label guide uses these measurements for a shaped template.

Oval bottles, tubes and deeply contoured jars also need an applied sample. For a jar, measure the lid and sidewall separately; see our cosmetic jar label guide.

Reserve space for copy and application

Place the brand and product name where they remain visible from the front. Keep the approved ingredients, directions, batch area and barcode legible at actual print size. If a retail barcode is required, ask the barcode owner or printer to verify the symbol, quiet zones and position on the curved package; GS1's barcode quality checklist identifies these as scan-quality factors.

For machine-applied rolls, confirm unwind direction, core size, maximum roll diameter and the gap between labels with the applicator operator. For hand application, say whether sheets or rolls are preferred. These production details affect the quote even when the printed artwork is identical.

What to send for a useful size and price review

Send a front and side photo of the filled container, its material, proposed label width and height, wrap gap if applicable, quantity per artwork version, application method and destination country. Include the current artwork or approved copy. A digital proof checks layout; approve the final size, adhesion and handling on a physical applied sample.

Then compare the relevant product route: cosmetic bottle labels, wash care bottle labels or candle jar labels. The bottle measurement method is a starting point, not a promise that one material or adhesive suits every package.
